# EU AI Act <Badge type="warning" text="to learn" />

## The idea

The EU AI Act (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689) regulates AI systems by **risk**: the higher the
risk to health, safety or fundamental rights, the stricter the rules. It applies to Swiss
companies too, if they put AI systems on the EU market or their outputs are used in the EU.

*A study summary, not legal advice. Check the current dates, since a 2025 EU simplification
proposal may shift some deadlines.*

## Look-up

| Risk level | Examples | Obligations |
|---|---|---|
| Unacceptable | Social scoring, manipulative techniques, untargeted scraping of facial images, emotion recognition at work and school | Banned |
| High | Recruitment and HR, credit scoring, education, critical infrastructure, law enforcement | Risk management, data governance, documentation, logging, human oversight, accuracy, conformity assessment |
| Limited | Chatbots, AI-generated content | Transparency: tell people they are dealing with AI, and label generated content |
| Minimal | Spam filters, most internal analytics | None beyond existing law |

- **Roles:** the *provider* builds or places the system on the market; the *deployer* uses
  it. Most obligations fall on providers.
- **General-purpose AI models** (foundation models) have their own documentation and
  copyright duties, with more for models with systemic risk.
- **Timeline:** in force since 1 August 2024. Bans and the AI literacy duty since
  2 February 2025, general-purpose AI rules since 2 August 2025, and most high-risk rules
  from 2 August 2026.
- **Fines** reach €35 million or 7% of global turnover for banned practices.
- **Switzerland** has no copy of the AI Act. In 2025 the Federal Council chose to ratify the
  Council of Europe AI convention and adapt existing laws by sector.
